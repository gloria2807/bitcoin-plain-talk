# Consensus

**Category:** Network

## Plain English

Agreement among the majority of nodes and miners on what the valid state of the blockchain is — without any central authority making that decision.

## Analogy

Imagine a town where everyone keeps their own copy of the town records. Before a new record is added, most townspeople must agree it's legitimate. Bitcoin works the same way, thousands of independent computers (nodes) must agree that a transaction is valid before it becomes permanent.

## In Context

Bitcoin reaches consensus through proof of work.

What this means: Miners compete to solve puzzles and earn the right to add a block. Once a block is added, nodes verify that block. If 51% or more of the network's computing power agrees the block is valid, consensus is reached and the block becomes part of the permanent record.

## Why It Matters

Consensus is what makes Bitcoin decentralised and trustless. No single authority can change the rules or rewrite history. An attacker would need to control more than 51% of the network's computing power to lie, a task so expensive and difficult that it's not worth attempting. This is why Bitcoin is considered secure.
