# Peer-to-Peer

**Category:** Network

## Plain English

A system where two people can transact directly with each other. There's no bank, no middleman, no permission required.

## Analogy

Sending money through a bank is like sending a parcel through a courier company, it goes through a third party who can delay it, charge fees, or refuse to deliver it. Peer-to-peer is like handing that parcel directly to the person. In Lagos, think of the difference between sending money through a mobile money agent versus handing someone cash directly at the market. Bitcoin is the digital version of that direct handoff, anyone, anywhere, can send to anyone else without asking permission.

## In Context

Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System.

This is the subtitle of Bitcoin's original whitepaper, written by Satoshi Nakamoto in 2008. It describes exactly what Bitcoin was designed to be, a way for people to send money directly to each other over the internet, the same way you send an email, without needing a bank in the middle.

## Why It Matters

Peer-to-peer means no single company or government controls who can send or receive Bitcoin. A person in a country with no banking access can receive Bitcoin from anywhere in the world, no account is needed, no approval is required. This is one of the most powerful properties of Bitcoin, especially for people in places where financial infrastructure is limited or unreliable.
