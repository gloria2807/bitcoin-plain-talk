# Inflation

**Category:** Economy

## Plain English

The gradual loss of a currency's purchasing power over time, meaning the same amount of money buys less than it used to.

## Analogy

Imagine a jug of milk cost one coin last year, but this year it costs two coins even though nothing about the milk changed. That's inflation: your money didn't lose the coin, but the coin itself buys less. It's like the ground slowly sinking under your feet while you stand still, you haven't moved but you're lower than before.

## In Context

*"Bitcoin's fixed supply is often described as a hedge against inflation."*

**What this means:** Because only 21 million bitcoin will ever exist, its supply can't be inflated the way government currencies can be by printing more money. Some people hold Bitcoin hoping it will preserve their purchasing power better than fiat currency over the long run.

## Why It Matters

Inflation quietly erodes savings held in cash. Over decades, even modest inflation can significantly reduce what your money is worth. This is one of the main economic arguments people make in favor of Bitcoin: a monetary system with a capped, transparent supply schedule that no government can alter.
