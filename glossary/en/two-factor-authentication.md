# Two-Factor Authentication

**Category:** Security

## Plain English

A security method that requires two separate proofs of identity to log in or approve an action, usually a password plus a code from your phone, instead of relying on a password alone.

## Analogy

Think of it like needing both a key and a fingerprint scan to open a safe. Even if someone steals your key (your password), they still can't get in without your fingerprint too (the second factor), which they don't have.

## In Context

*"Enable two-factor authentication on your exchange account to protect it from hackers."*

**What this means:** In addition to a password, the person should set up a second layer of verification, like a code generated by an authenticator app, so that a stolen password alone isn't enough for someone to access their account.

## Why It Matters

Exchange accounts and custodial services are common targets for hackers, and passwords alone are often leaked in data breaches. Two-factor authentication adds a critical extra barrier, though it protects accounts on custodial platforms, not a self-custody wallet's private keys directly.
