# Difficulty Adjustment

**Category:** Network

## Plain English

An automatic mechanism that keeps Bitcoin block times close to 10 minutes, by making the mining puzzle harder when more miners join the network, and easier when miners leave.

## Analogy

Imagine a school exam where the pass mark adjusts based on how many students are sitting for it. If a thousand brilliant students join, the pass mark goes up so only the right number pass. If most students drop out, the pass mark goes down so the class can still graduate on time. Bitcoin does this every 2,016 blocks, roughly every two weeks, automatically recalibrating how hard the mining puzzle is based on how much computing power is on the network.

## In Context

After the last halving, some miners shut down their machines, triggering a difficulty adjustment downward.

When mining becomes less profitable, for example after a halving cuts the block reward, some miners switch off. With fewer miners competing, blocks would arrive too slowly. Bitcoin automatically detects this and reduces the mining difficulty so the 10-minute rhythm is restored.

## Why It Matters

Difficulty adjustment is what makes Bitcoin reliable. No matter how many miners join or leave, Bitcoin keeps producing blocks at a steady pace. It means Bitcoin cannot be overwhelmed by a sudden surge of miners, and it cannot slow to a halt if miners leave. It's the self-balancing heartbeat of the network.
