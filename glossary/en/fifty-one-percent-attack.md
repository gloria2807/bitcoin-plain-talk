# 51% Attack

**Category:** Mining

## Plain English

A theoretical attack where a single miner or group controls more than half of the network's total mining power, potentially letting them manipulate which transactions get confirmed.

## Analogy

Imagine a jury of a thousand people voting on facts, where the majority vote decides the truth. If one group secretly controlled 501 of those jurors, they could outvote everyone else and force through a decision, even a false one. A 51% attack works similarly: whoever controls the majority of mining power can, in theory, overrule the honest majority of the network for a period of time.

## In Context

*"Bitcoin's massive hashrate makes a 51% attack extremely expensive and impractical."*

**What this means:** To pull off a 51% attack against Bitcoin, someone would need to acquire more mining hardware and electricity than the rest of the entire global network combined, a cost running into billions of dollars, making it economically irrational.

## Why It Matters

A 51% attack could, at most, let an attacker reverse their own recent transactions or block certain transactions temporarily, it cannot let them steal other people's bitcoin or change old, deeply buried blocks. Bitcoin's enormous, globally distributed hashrate is what makes this attack so costly it's never been successfully carried out against it.
