# Selfish Mining

**Category:** Mining

## Plain English

A strategy where a miner secretly withholds a newly found block instead of broadcasting it immediately, hoping to gain an unfair advantage over competing miners.

## Analogy

Imagine a runner in a relay race who secretly finds a shortcut but doesn't tell their teammates, instead using the hidden advantage only when it benefits them most, letting competitors continue running the long way while they get ahead unnoticed. Selfish mining works similarly: a miner hides their lead to try to waste other miners' effort.

## In Context

*"Researchers have studied selfish mining as a theoretical threat to Bitcoin's fairness."*

**What this means:** Academics have modeled scenarios where a large mining pool could, in theory, earn more than their fair share of rewards by strategically delaying when they reveal newly mined blocks to the rest of the network.

## Why It Matters

Selfish mining is mostly a theoretical concern requiring a very large share of network hashrate to be effective, and Bitcoin's design includes incentives that make it risky and often unprofitable in practice. It's a useful concept for understanding why decentralized, widely distributed mining power matters for fairness.
