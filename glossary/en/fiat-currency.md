# Fiat Currency

**Category:** Economy

## Plain English

Government-issued money, like the dollar, euro, or shilling, that has value because a government says it does, not because it's backed by gold or any other physical asset.

## Analogy

Think of fiat currency like a restaurant voucher printed by the restaurant itself. It's worth something because the restaurant says it will honor it, and everyone trusts that promise. But the restaurant can print as many vouchers as it wants, and if it prints too many, each one buys less food. Bitcoin was designed to work differently: nobody can just print more of it.

## In Context

*"I converted my fiat savings into Bitcoin because I don't trust the currency to hold its value."*

**What this means:** The person exchanged government-issued money (dollars, shillings, etc.) for Bitcoin because they're worried that inflation or money printing will erode the purchasing power of their fiat savings over time.

## Why It Matters

Understanding fiat currency helps explain why Bitcoin was created. Fiat money supply can be expanded by central banks and governments, which can lead to inflation. Bitcoin has a fixed, predictable supply that no single authority can change, which is why many people see it as an alternative or hedge to fiat currency.
