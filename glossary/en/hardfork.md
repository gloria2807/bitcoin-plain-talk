# Hard Fork

**Category:** Technology

## Plain English

A change to Bitcoin's rules that is not backwards-compatible. Meaning nodes that don't upgrade are permanently separated from those that do, creating two different chains.

## Analogy

Imagine a road that divides into two paths with no way back to the original. Cars on the old path can't travel on the new one, and vice versa. A hard fork is that split, once it happens, the two sides of the network go their own separate ways. This is how Bitcoin Cash was created in 2017: a group disagreed with Bitcoin's direction, changed the rules in a way that was incompatible, and the network split into two coins.

## In Context

Bitcoin Cash was created through a hard fork of Bitcoin.

A group of developers and miners changed Bitcoin's rules in a way that the original Bitcoin network wouldn't accept. From that point, both chains continued independently, one as Bitcoin (BTC), one as Bitcoin Cash (BCH). Anyone who held Bitcoin before the split received an equal amount of both coins.

## Why It Matters

Hard forks are permanent. Once the split happens, the two networks are completely separate; different coins, different communities, different rules. This is why major changes to Bitcoin are proposed carefully and take years of discussion. An accidental or contentious hard fork can fracture a community and create confusion about which chain is the "real" one.
