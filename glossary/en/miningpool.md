# Mining Pool

**Category:** Network

## Plain English

A group of Bitcoin miners who combine their computing power to improve their chances of earning a block reward, then share the payout among themselves.

## Analogy

Imagine ten friends each buying one lottery ticket. Each person's chance of winning alone is small. But if they pool their money together, buy ten tickets, and agree to split any winnings, their combined chances are much better and the income becomes more predictable. A mining pool works the same way: solo miners team up so that instead of waiting months or years for a payout, they earn smaller but more regular rewards.

## In Context

Most Bitcoin miners today operate through a mining pool rather than mining solo.

Solo mining is possible, but the competition is so fierce that an individual miner could go a very long time without finding a block. Pools let miners earn steady income proportional to how much computing power they contribute, even if the pool, not the individual, is the one that actually finds the block.

## Why It Matters

Mining pools make Bitcoin mining financially practical for smaller miners. However, they also raise concerns: if one pool controls too much of the network's computing power, it could theoretically threaten Bitcoin's decentralisation. This is why the Bitcoin community watches pool size closely and why spreading hashrate across many pools is healthier for the network.
