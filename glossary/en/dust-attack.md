# Dust Attack

**Category:** Security

## Plain English

An attempt by someone to compromise privacy by sending tiny, near-worthless amounts of bitcoin, called dust, to many wallet addresses, then tracking how that dust moves to try to link addresses to a single owner.

## Analogy

Imagine someone secretly slipping a tiny tracking dye onto several different packages you own, then watching to see which delivery trucks pick them up and where they all end up. If several packages end up at the same address, they've learned those packages, and their owners, are connected.

## In Context

*"I noticed a tiny, unexpected deposit in my wallet, that could be a dust attack."*

**What this means:** Someone may have sent a small amount of bitcoin to this person's address specifically to see if it later gets combined with other funds in a future transaction, which would reveal a link between multiple addresses controlled by the same wallet.

## Why It Matters

If unspent dust gets combined with other coins in a future transaction, it can reveal that separate addresses belong to the same person, undermining privacy. Many modern wallets now flag suspicious dust and let users avoid spending it.
