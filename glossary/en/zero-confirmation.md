# Zero-Confirmation

**Category:** Transactions

## Plain English

A transaction that has been broadcast to the network but hasn't yet been included in a mined block, meaning it's still technically reversible.

## Analogy

Think of it like a check that's been handed over but hasn't cleared the bank yet. It looks like payment has happened, but until the bank actually processes it, there's a small window where things could still go wrong.

## In Context

*"That coffee shop accepts zero-confirmation payments for small amounts to keep things fast."*

**What this means:** For a small purchase, the merchant is accepting the transaction as soon as it's broadcast, without waiting for it to be confirmed in a block, trading a small amount of risk for a much faster checkout experience.

## Why It Matters

Zero-confirmation transactions are convenient but carry a real, if usually small, risk of double-spending, especially for larger amounts. Most merchants and services wait for at least one confirmation before considering a payment final for anything valuable.
