# Full Node

**Category:** Network

## Plain English

A computer that downloads the entire Bitcoin blockchain and independently checks every transaction and block against Bitcoin's rules, without trusting anyone else to do it.

## Analogy

Think of a community that keeps its own copy of the village land registry. Instead of asking the government office every time someone wants to confirm who owns a plot, the community checks their own records. A full node is that community record-keeper, it doesn't trust banks, governments, or other computers. It checks everything itself, from the very first Bitcoin transaction to the latest one.

## In Context

Running a full node means you don't have to trust anyone, you verify everything yourself.

When you use a mobile wallet or an exchange, you're trusting them to tell you the truth about your balance and Bitcoin's rules. A full node trusts no one, it has its own complete copy of the blockchain and enforces the rules independently. This is the most sovereign way to use Bitcoin.

## Why It Matters

Full nodes are the backbone of Bitcoin's decentralisation. The more people running them across the world, the harder it is for anyone - governments, miners, developers - to change Bitcoin's rules without broad agreement. You don't need to run one to use Bitcoin, but the network is healthier when more people do.
